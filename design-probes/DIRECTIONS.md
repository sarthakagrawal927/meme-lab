# Meme Lab visual direction set

## Search frame

Meme Lab is an owner-operated local experiment, not a marketing site. Its primary job is to lock one situation, compare two anonymous selector conditions, capture a taste judgment and reveal the experimental difference. The redesign must preserve the no-dependency plain HTML/CSS/JavaScript stack, all existing accessibility and privacy boundaries, 390–1440 px responsiveness, and the complete catalogue/research/history surfaces.

Desired qualities: simple, airy, friendly, legible, fast and lightly playful. Anti-qualities: oversized landing-page hero, dense control-room chrome, severe editorial styling, beige lifestyle branding, ornamental meme imagery, or controls hidden beneath introductory copy.

No external product visual system was copied. These probes use project-native composition because the blind A/B review is the defining interaction and no existing component library is needed to demonstrate it.

## Anti-reference

Two things are now explicit anti-references. The rejected v0.2 surface spends the first viewport on an oversized slogan and catalogue count, then compresses the workflow into generic beige cards. The first probe round overcorrected into dark control-room, severe editorial and cinematic studio systems. The redesign must show the real experiment immediately without feeling heavy.

## Shared direction principles

1. Put the situation and anonymous A/B judgment in the first viewport.
2. Treat experiment constraints as visible state, not explanatory footnotes.
3. Make abstention visually equal to a meme result.
4. Hide method identity and rationales without hiding the candidate names needed for judgment.
5. Give catalogue, sources and history a secondary application-navigation role.

## Direction A — Soft Workspace

- **Purpose / audience / screen job:** make one experiment easy to understand, run and judge without visual ceremony.
- **Visual thesis:** a calm light workspace with just enough lilac and sky color to make state obvious.
- **Layout:** compact header, contextual setup column, generous side-by-side A/B review area and a quiet reveal action.
- **Typography:** clean contemporary sans with restrained weight and comfortable line-height.
- **Semantic color:** white and cool-gray surfaces, lilac for the active experiment, sky for condition-neutral information, mint for positive judgment and coral for negative judgment.
- **Interaction thesis:** the current step is always obvious and the next action stays close to the content.
- **Signature:** a soft lilac experiment rail connecting setup, comparison and reveal.
- **Deliberate risk:** the calm tone may feel too restrained if the color accents become too timid.

## Direction B — Friendly Cards

- **Purpose / audience / screen job:** make the experiment feel approachable and enjoyable while preserving the blind comparison.
- **Visual thesis:** a warm, centered flow made from a few large, colorful paper-like cards.
- **Layout:** narrow centered column, situation card first, two large result cards below, then a single reveal card.
- **Typography:** rounded sans with friendly labels and large readable candidate names.
- **Semantic color:** peach for context, butter yellow and blush for anonymous arms, mint for completion and lavender for method notes.
- **Interaction thesis:** complete one obvious step at a time with generous targets and little peripheral chrome.
- **Signature:** each workflow step has its own soft color field rather than another bordered panel.
- **Deliberate risk:** the playful warmth must not make research history or diagnostics feel toy-like.

## Direction C — Clean Canvas

- **Purpose / audience / screen job:** support fast repeated experiments with a crisp, lightweight application structure.
- **Visual thesis:** a white working canvas with small blue, mint and yellow blocks acting as navigational landmarks.
- **Layout:** light sidebar, slim top bar, full-width context strip and structured A/B comparison rows.
- **Typography:** neutral UI sans with compact metadata and strong result names.
- **Semantic color:** white canvas, pale blue for navigation/current state, mint for positive judgment, yellow for attention and rose for negative judgment.
- **Interaction thesis:** scan vertically, compare horizontally and keep secondary areas one click away.
- **Signature:** the two arms read as clean comparison rows rather than dashboard cards.
- **Deliberate risk:** the structured layout can drift toward generic productivity software if the experiment rail loses personality.

## Decision needed

The owner must select A, B or C before the production interface is replaced. A hybrid should be requested explicitly and name the primary direction plus the one element to borrow.
