# Meme Lab professional redesign directions

All three directions keep the same product behavior: one full-sentence input, one best result, four scored backups, serious-input abstention, the searchable 3,000-item collection, and the documented method. They remove the edge doodles, candy-colored example tiles, oversized marketing headline, and rounded-card repetition that currently make the tool feel toy-like.

## A — Cutting Room

- Purpose: make matching feel like a deliberate media-selection workflow for an owner repeatedly testing reactions.
- Visual thesis: a charcoal film-editing workspace with one warm result surface, disciplined blue action color, and compact operational metadata.
- Layout and type: transcript/input at left; selected reaction and a horizontal backup strip at right; neutral system sans with large editorial result names.
- Semantic colors: graphite workspace, ivory content, blue actions, green ready state, muted gray evidence.
- Interaction thesis: the input remains pinned while results update in the adjacent viewer.
- Signature: each ranked reaction reads like a frame in a five-shot editing strip.
- Deliberate risk: the dark workspace is highly professional but could feel heavier than a lightweight personal tool.

## B — Reference Desk

- Purpose: make repeated matching fast, trustworthy, and legible without losing approachability.
- Visual thesis: a restrained navy-and-cloud application with one blue action system and muted green for successful fit.
- Layout and type: compact left navigation; sentence composer and result viewer share the first viewport; backups form a quiet evidence grid.
- Semantic colors: navy navigation, white working surfaces, powder-blue media field, cobalt actions, green fit state, slate metadata.
- Interaction thesis: read left to right—describe the moment, inspect the answer, record whether it fits.
- Signature: the sidebar keeps the catalogue scale visible while the working area behaves like a serious reference tool.
- Deliberate risk: it can drift toward generic SaaS unless the result viewer and evidence details stay distinctly meme-specific.

## C — Contact Sheet

- Purpose: make comparing one primary reaction and four alternatives feel fast and culturally literate.
- Visual thesis: an editorial image desk with black rules, off-white stock, a single vermilion action color, and tightly composed media.
- Layout and type: a compact sentence strip sits above one large primary frame and a four-item ranked contact sheet.
- Semantic colors: neutral paper, black structure, vermilion rank/action, gray secondary evidence.
- Interaction thesis: run once, scan the whole ranked set, then confirm the primary without opening another layer.
- Signature: the five results form one contact sheet instead of five disconnected cards.
- Deliberate risk: the editorial grid is memorable but needs careful mobile reflow and strong input focus.

## Recommendation

Choose **B — Reference Desk**. It best balances the owner's earlier request for simplicity and pretty color with the new requirement that the product feel credible rather than playful. It also scales cleanly across Collection and How It Works without turning those pages into marketing surfaces.
